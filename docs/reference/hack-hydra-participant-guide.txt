01 / OVERVIEW                                                                                       HydraDB



Welcome to Hack Hydra
HydraDB is a graph database built for AI applications, using object storage to stay fast
and cost-efficient. From August 12-20, 2026 we are open-sourcing HydraDB
and inviting developers globally to build across three technical tracks: enterprise
knowledge, coding agents and agent memory, while competing for up to $10,000
in prizes.
Every track is a problem where relationships, structure and connected context actually
matter, and none of them is something a vector database handles well. Pick one, build
with HydraDB, and submit something working by August 20.

Enterprise Context + Ontology
CORE IDEA

Build an ontology out of real enterprise applications. You get roughly half a million
documents drawn from nine different sources, with all the noise an actual company has:
misfiled documents, near-duplicates, and statements that flatly contradict each other.
Your job is to turn that into a clean, queryable ontology in HydraDB and then answer
questions ranging from simple lookups to multi-hop reasoning, conflict resolution, and
correctly recognising when the answer just isn't in there.

 Slack       Gmail     Linear     Google Drive   HubSpot    Fireflies       GitHub     Jira


 Confluence




   THE HARD PART


   Extraction is the easy part now that LLMs are cheap. The hard part is entity
   resolution and ontology alignment: deciding that “Sam”, “@soham” and “S.
   Ratnaparkhi” are one person, and figuring out which of two contradictory
   statements to trust.

Memory + Context Retrieval
CORE IDEA

Make your own mem0, and ace the benchmarks. Build an agent memory layer for cross-
session continuity.
Your system processes chat histories spanning 30 to 40 sessions and 115,000 tokens per
question. It has to synthesize facts across sessions, keep chronological order, and track
information that was overwritten along the way.

   THE HARD PART


   Long-context models drop 30 to 60% in accuracy here, mostly by failing at
   abstention. Knowing when the answer simply isn't in the history, and saying so
   instead of inventing one.

05 / RULES                                                                                         HydraDB


Keep it simple
Start on August 12, use HydraDB properly, and keep everything open to inspection.


Build it during the nine days
Work on your submission begins on or after August 12, 2026. Reusing general-purpose libraries,
frameworks and templates is fine, as long as the project you submit was substantially built during the
hackathon.

Use HydraDB properly
HydraDB has to be meaningfully integrated into the project and contribute to core functionality. Be
ready to say where it is used and what the project would lose without it.

Teams of 1 to 4
Every member has to be listed on the submission form, and you can only be on one team.

Multiple tracks are allowed
Teams may submit to multiple tracks, but each submission must be a meaningfully distinct project. A
team may receive only one Track Winner prize, and stays eligible for Best Use of HydraDB either way.

Open-source tools are allowed
Libraries, frameworks, APIs, public datasets and AI coding assistants are all fair game. Credit anything
you borrowed in the README. Commit history inherited from upstream repos or dependencies does not
count against you.

Keep the repository public and licensed
Judges have to be able to open it without requesting access, and it needs an open-source license.

Submit by August 20
Everything goes through the official form by 11:59 PM PT. Late submissions are not accepted unless
HydraDB announces an official extension, so open your own repo, video and demo links before you
submit.
